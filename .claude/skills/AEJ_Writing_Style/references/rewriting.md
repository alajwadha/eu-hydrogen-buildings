# Rewriting moves

Twelve mechanical operations that convert essayistic prose into Applied Energy's voice.
Each is stated as a trigger you can spot, an operation you can apply, and worked examples
using real sentences from a working-paper draft.

Apply these when you have a specific sentence in front of you. For the reasoning behind
them, see `wording.md`; for what belongs in each section, see `anatomy.md`.

## 1. Promote the thing to subject

**Trigger.** The sentence opens with an authorial gesture, an abstract placeholder, or a
prepositional scene-setter that buries what the sentence is about.

**Operation.** Ask what thing the sentence concerns and put it in the subject slot.

| Draft | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *On the levelised-cost test the outcome settles on the cost of holding the molecule between seasons.* | *The levelised-cost comparison is governed by the cost of seasonal storage.* |
| *We price that reinforcement inside the heat pump's delivered cost.* | *The heat pump's delivered cost includes distribution-grid reinforcement.* |
| *What the dispatch tests deliver is a bound.* | *The dispatch tests deliver a bound on hydrogen's heating share.* |

*We* survives where the act is genuinely authorial: *we propose*, *we develop*, *we find*,
*we incorporated*. It does not survive as navigation.

## 2. Fold the consequence into a trailing participle

**Trigger.** Two sentences where the second states what follows from the first, often
opening *So*, *This means*, *As a result* or *Therefore*.

**Operation.** Attach the consequence to the first sentence with an *-ing* clause.

Verb pool: *leading to · resulting in · yielding · allowing · enabling · supporting ·
highlighting · emphasizing · indicating · suggesting · reflecting · representing ·
necessitating · providing · thereby reducing · contributing to*.

| Draft | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *Space heating draws three-quarters of its energy in winter while electrolytic production is flatter. So the mismatch must be stored.* | *Space heating draws approximately three-quarters of its energy between October and March, whereas electrolytic production is considerably flatter, necessitating seasonal storage of the resulting mismatch.* |
| *The peaker runs a few hundred hours a year. It therefore cannot recover its capital from market rent.* | *The peaker operates only a few hundred hours per year, preventing recovery of its capital from market rent.* |
| *Cheap storage absorbs the seasonal mismatch. This is why hydrogen's base-load case concentrates in cavern markets.* | *Cheap storage absorbs the seasonal mismatch, concentrating hydrogen's base-load case in the salt-cavern markets.* |

Do not apply this twice in one sentence; two trailing participles in series become hard to
parse.

## 3. Split the sentence doing three jobs

**Trigger.** A sentence with two or more subordinate clauses, each of which could stand
alone. Usually over 40 words.

**Operation.** Find the second verb doing independent work and break there. Then link the
halves with a demonstrative or a connective.

| Draft | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *Hydrogen was priced as a system operator prices it, on short-run marginal cost in dispatch, which bounds the heating share it could hold by where it wins the winter-peak merit order.* | *Hydrogen is priced on short-run marginal cost, as a system operator would dispatch it. **This bounds** its heating share to the markets in which it wins the winter-peak merit order.* |
| *The demand reconstruction agrees with the Hotmaps regional baseline to −0.8 per cent across the aggregate and to within ±25 per cent in 28 of the 29 countries, which is the stricter vintage-matched comparison that rescales our stock on dwelling growth alone.* | *The demand reconstruction agrees with the Hotmaps regional baseline to −0.8% across the aggregate and to within ±25% in 28 of the 29 countries. **This comparison** is vintage-matched, rescaling the stock on dwelling growth alone.* |

## 4. Name the previous sentence

**Trigger.** A sentence beginning *That*, *It*, *Which*, or launching a new noun with no
link back.

**Operation.** Open with *This* or *These* plus a noun that classifies what just happened.

Noun pool: *approach · finding · result · discrepancy · trend · pattern · behaviour ·
choice · method · gap · effect · difference · constraint · challenge · limitation ·
condition · asymmetry · mechanism*.

| Draft | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *That is the same condition that decides the peaking arena.* | ***This condition** also determines the outcome in the peaking arena.* |
| *It is why the two tests agree.* | ***This mechanism** explains the agreement between the two tests.* |
| *The count runs from 13 to 29. The hydrogen price path is what moves it.* | *The count runs from 13 to 29 across the hydrogen price paths. **This sensitivity** is driven by the price path rather than by the policy scenario.* |

The gain is not only cohesion. Choosing the noun forces you to decide what the previous
sentence *was*, and the reader gets that classification free.

## 5. Mark the logical relation

**Trigger.** Two clauses in contrast, concession or consequence, with the relation carried
only by juxtaposition or by a semicolon.

**Operation.** Insert the explicit connective. Contrast inside a sentence takes *whereas*;
contrast across sentences takes *However,* or *In contrast,*.

| Draft | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *Continental models report hydrogen's role at national resolution; building-stock assessments fix its share exogenously.* | *Continental models resolve hydrogen's role in building heat only at national resolution, **whereas** building-stock assessments fix its share exogenously.* |
| *Neither gives a hydrogen peaker's standalone capital-recovery position market by market.* | ***However**, neither provides a standalone capital-recovery position for a hydrogen peaker at the market level.* |
| *The count is fragile. It runs from 13 to 29.* | *The count is fragile, **ranging from** 13 to 29 across the hydrogen price paths.* |

## 6. Convert *not X but Y*

**Trigger.** Any *not X but Y*, *X and not Y*, or *rather than* used for emphasis.

**Operation.** Either restate as a plain comparative with *rather than*, or use the
corpus's deny-then-supply pattern when the misreading is worth naming.

| Draft | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *The count turns on the hydrogen price path and not the policy scenario.* | *The count is determined by the hydrogen price path **rather than by** the policy scenario.* |
| *The third test is decisive rather than incidental.* | *The capital-recovery test **is the binding constraint**.* |
| *Its widest count is a ceiling on a residential gas tariff rather than a competitive score.* | ***However, this count should not be interpreted as** a competitive score. **Rather, it represents** a ceiling set by the residential gas tariff.* |

That last form, *should not be interpreted as X. Rather, it indicates Y*, is a corpus
pattern and is worth using wherever a referee might misread a result.

## 7. Flatten repetition-with-development

**Trigger.** A phrase repeated inside a sentence for rhetorical effect.

**Operation.** State the point once, plainly.

| Draft | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *On the levelised-cost test the outcome settles, and it settles on the cost of holding the molecule between seasons.* | *The levelised-cost outcome is determined by the cost of seasonal storage.* |
| *The power role is the clearest case. The firm peaking fleet is about 278 GW.* | *The firm peaking fleet required by the electrified heat load is approximately 278 GW.* |
| *Hydrogen wins to run, not to build.* | *Hydrogen is competitive on operating cost but does not recover its capital.* |

## 8. Attach the number to the claim

**Trigger.** A qualitative claim whose supporting number sits in a different sentence, or
nowhere.

**Operation.** Bring the number inside, in parentheses or apposition, with its driver
attached via *driven by* or *attributed to*.

| Draft | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *No hydrogen peaker recovers its capital from market rent in any country or scenario we run.* | *No hydrogen peaker recovers its capital from market rent in any country or scenario, **with capacity-weighted recovery reaching only 9%** across the markets that build.* |
| *The count is fragile across price paths.* | *The count is fragile, **ranging from 13 to 29 of 29** across the hydrogen price paths.* |
| *Five of those seven are salt-cavern markets, including the four widest.* | *Five of the seven are salt-cavern markets, **including the four widest gaps (€21–38/MWh)**.* |

This also applies to prior work. A claim about a cited study should carry that study's
figure: *reduced the coefficient of variation by 20.8% [16]*, not *found benefits*.

## 9. Collapse parallel results with *respectively*

**Trigger.** Two or three sentences reporting the same quantity for different cases.

**Operation.** One sentence, values in order, closed with *respectively*.

| Draft | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *The heat pump delivers €118.5/MWh. The hydrogen boiler delivers €122.8/MWh. The gas boiler delivers €132.7/MWh.* | *The best heat pump, hydrogen boiler and gas boiler deliver €118.5, €122.8 and €132.7/MWh of useful heat, **respectively**.* |
| *Under Current Policies the count is 0. Under Stated Policies it is 4. Under Net Zero it is 9. Under H2 Push it is 16.* | *The building winter-peak count is 0, 4, 9 and 16 under Current Policies, Stated Policies, Net Zero and H2 Push, **respectively**.* |

## 10. Give the figure its sentence

**Trigger.** A finding stated with `(Fig. N)` trailing in parentheses.

**Operation.** Make the float the grammatical subject of its own sentence, then state the
finding.

| Draft | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *The best heat pump is cheaper in 22 of 29 countries (Fig. 4).* | ***Fig. 4 presents** the 2050 levelised cost of heat by country. The best heat pump is the cheaper clean option in 22 of the 29 markets.* |
| *Recovery spans 5 to 40 per cent across the five markets that build (Fig. 6).* | ***Fig. 6 shows** the capital-recovery position of the hydrogen peaker by market. Recovery ranges from 5% to 40% across the five markets that build.* |

Validation belongs immediately after, before interpretation: *…**show good agreement
with** the Hotmaps regional baseline [n]*.

## 11. Convert an argumentative heading

**Trigger.** A section or subsection heading that states a finding.

**Operation.** Replace with a noun phrase naming the content. Move the finding into the
first sentence beneath it.

| Draft heading | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *On levelised cost, hydrogen holds base load in seven markets, where cheap storage and heavy electricity taxes meet* | *Levelised cost of heat* |
| *Repriced as a dispatchable option, hydrogen's power-sector win is confined to the cavern markets* | *Merit-order dispatch in the power sector* |
| *Even where it wins to run, the peaker cannot recover its capital* | *Capital recovery of the hydrogen peaker* |
| *A sticky demand, a lever-driven mix and a policy-set emissions fan* | *Heat demand, technology mix and emissions* |
| *A least-cost cross-check, and where the carbon price alone falls short* | *Cost-optimal pathway* |
| *Switzerland: both tests close against hydrogen* | *Switzerland* |

## 12. Recover a hanging concession

**Trigger.** A limitation or caveat stated and left standing.

**Operation.** Follow it in the same or the next sentence with what the study nonetheless
achieves, or with what the limitation does not affect.

| Draft | Rewritten |
|---|---|
| *Those power-sector figures rest on one realisation of a single synthetic weather year, which is the largest simplification here.* | *The power-sector figures rest on one realisation of a single synthetic weather year, which is the largest simplification in this study. **Nevertheless**, the ranking of markets is preserved across the sensitivity cases, **indicating that** the conclusions are robust to the weather realisation.* |
| *The comparison prices hydrogen green where a detailed assessment argues it would be blue.* | *The comparison prices hydrogen as green, **whereas** a detailed assessment of the German heating case argues it would in practice be blue [n]. **This assumption is conservative**, since the blue route would widen the heat pump's margin by €31/MWh.* |

The pattern in the corpus is unvarying: *While grounded in a specific geological setting,
the methodology and insights extend more broadly…*, *However, despite this constraint, the
study effectively utilises existing data…*

## Order of application

Working sentence by sentence is slow and produces uneven results. Work by move instead,
sweeping the whole manuscript once per move, in this order:

1. **Headings** (move 11). Fast, mechanical, and it changes how the paper scans
   immediately.
2. **Figure sentences** (move 10). Also mechanical, and it fixes the results section's
   voice in one pass.
3. **Long sentences** (move 3). Split anything past about 45 words.
4. **Consequences** (move 2). Convert *so*, *therefore*, *this means* pairs.
5. **Logical relations** (move 5) and **rhetorical constructions** (moves 6 and 7).
6. **Cohesion** (move 4). Read for sentences that start cold.
7. **Numbers** (moves 8 and 9).
8. **Concessions** (move 12). Last, because it needs the surrounding text settled.

Then re-run the project's numerical gate. Moves 8 and 9 touch numbers directly, and moves
1 and 3 rewrite sentences that a gate may be matching on.
