# Applied Energy phrasebank

Every line below is quoted or lightly trimmed from the 14-paper corpus. Organised by the
job the sentence is doing, so it can be used while writing rather than read once.

> **These are other people's published sentences.** Take the frame and leave the content.
> `This study presents…`, `However, no study has…`, `Fig. 5 shows the…` are field
> conventions and belong to nobody. Everything that fills them belongs to the author who
> wrote it: their subject, their adjectives, their explanation of their own result.
> Swapping the nouns in someone's clause is paraphrase, not style.
>
> After writing from a frame here, cover the frame and read what is left. If the remainder
> still describes the corpus author's system, rewrite it. If more than about eight
> consecutive words match a line below and they are not a standard field term, rewrite it.
> Where the idea itself is being used, cite them. See `SKILL.md`, *what may be copied and
> what may not*.

> arrived at an agreement marking the "beginning of the end" of the fossil fuel era [1].

> The urgency of addressing pressing environmental challenges led the parties at the
> COP 28 to identify effective strategies for the retirement of fossil fuel based power
> plants, including acceleration of renewable hydrogen production and utilisation [1,2].

> With the escalating challenges of environmental pollution, rising sea levels, climate
> change, and the energy crisis, developing and deploying efficient, clean, and
> sustainable energy sources have become a central priority in the 21st century [1–3].

> The globally increasing impacts of climate change and the pressing need for

## 4. Stating the gap

Direct and unembarrassed. Several papers use the word *gap* outright.

> Nonetheless, a critical methodological limitation pervades this existing literature:
> the ubiquitous reliance on static, historical meteorological data and deterministic
> cost models [18]. By assuming climate stationarity, these assessments fundamentally
> neglect the dynamic, multi-decadal interaction between anthropogenic climate change
> and shifting renewable energy resource availability [19].

> However, these studies did not assess the proximity of hydrogen production sites to
> various demand centers … which remain a research gap.

> A clear gap exists in understanding whether co-location of offshore wind, wave, and
> OFPV can systematically reduce costs on a global scale when key system interactions
> are considered, and what drives the potential cost reduction.

> While numerous studies analyze and evaluate individual aspects of hydrogen and
> hydrogen strategies, there remains a gap in quantitative analyses addressing holistic
> long-term hydrogen strategies and the factors influencing them.

> Despite the technical feasibility of renewable fuels, a major policy challenge remains:
> how to create regulatory environments and economic incentives that make synthetic
> alternatives like rLPG viable in competitive markets.

Frames: *Nonetheless, a critical X limitation pervades this existing literature: Y* ·
*However, these studies did not X* · *A clear gap exists in understanding whether X* ·
*While numerous studies X, there remains a gap in Y* · *Despite X, a major challenge
remains: Y*

## 8. Explaining why a modelling choice was made

Applied Energy justifies in the sentence that makes the choice.

> The "Unconstrained" scenario serves as a benchmark, representing a strategy where
> infrastructure and capacity decisions are made without policy-imposed constraints.
> This allows us to assess the alignment between the cost-optimal strategy identified in
> this study and current political objectives.

> While other scenarios like the NoH2WasteHeat and OffshoreH2 scenario were tested to
> analyze the change in supply dynamics and spatial distribution, the results are not
> further included in the discussion.

> Technology learning curves are disabled to isolate climate-induced effects.

> A project lifetime of 25 years and a real discount rate of 8% are used which lies
> within the range commonly adopted in offshore renewable energy techno-economic
> assessments, typically 5–10%, and reflects moderate investment risk assumptions.

> During the development of this study, the WKN had not yet been published, which is the
> reason it is not included in the scenario assumptions.

## 10. Wording a numeric result

> attributed to PV CAPEX, followed by 16.7% and 13.6% from LNH3 storage units and PEM,
> respectively.

> Internal heat exchange between coaxial pipes is found to account for up to 25% of
> radial heat loss in shallow sections, emphasizing the need for careful borehole
> configuration.

> As the annual injection–withdrawal frequency increases to five cycles, the LCOHS drops
> to $0.xx.

> Price-sensitive demand emerges in the energy conversion sector, where hydrogen use in
> combined heat and power plants increases at lower prices, reaching 138 TWh.

> A simplified production decline scenario illustrates an increase in the LCOH by nearly
> 70%.

> The results indicate that rLPG could substitute between 9.09 and 12.73 Mton of
> conventional fossil fuels by 2050, depending on the stringency of the transition

## 12. Framing the status of a claim

> It is clear that the most cost-effective modality will depend on local circumstances
> such as the availability of feedstocks and export/import infrastructure.

> It can thus be inferred that subsurface engineering costs predominantly influence
> capital expenditures.

> However, it is important to note that burning MeOH releases carbon, necessitating a
> carbon management process after cracking [8].

> From these findings we can conclude that according to our model, the optimal strategy
> consists of …

> However, these results should not be interpreted as policy-driven viability. Rather,
> they indicate that policy support can accelerate deployment for projects that are
> already near-commercial.

That last one is the **deny-then-supply** move: name the misreading explicitly, then give
the correct reading. An essayist would state only the correct reading.

## 13. Acknowledging a limitation

Acknowledge, then recover in the same breath.

> of hydrogen as an energy carrier. However, despite this constraint, the study
> effectively utilises existing data to generate robust, albeit approximate, models to
> achieve its objectives.

> While grounded in a specific geological setting, the methodology and insights extend
> more broadly to natural hydrogen projects worldwide.

> This is an important limitation considering the green hydrogen deployment mandate

## 17. Trailing-participle verbs

The signature connective. Collected from across the corpus:

*leading to* · *resulting in* · *yielding* · *allowing* · *enabling* · *supporting* ·
*highlighting* · *emphasizing* · *indicating* · *suggesting* · *reflecting* ·
*representing* · *necessitating* · *providing* · *offering* · *making it* · *thereby
reducing* · *contributing to* · *driven by* · *attributed to* · *depending on*

Examples in place:

> …has a long history of use as a fertiliser, **leading to** the development of
> infrastructures for its production, storage, and transportation at export scale [7].

> …burning MeOH releases carbon, **necessitating** a carbon management process after
> cracking [8].

> …waste-heat utilization generates economic benefits by lowering system costs and
> hydrogen transport needs, **highlighting** its value as an efficiency measure.

> …the resulting CSI ranges from 0 to 100, with higher values **indicating** more
> suitable locations.

> …enabling the comparison of potential sites over large coastal regions and
> **supporting** early-stage decision-making on optimal deployment areas.

> Hydrogen energy holds a prominent position among energy carriers due to its high energy
> density, zero-emission combustion, and versatility [6], **making it** a strategic
> choice for the energy transition in many countries.

## 18. Reporting market status and projections

> The current global installed capacity of offshore wind is approximately 83 GW, with an
> additional 48 GW under construction.

> Under high-ambition scenarios, the installed capacity of offshore wind is projected to
> increase to 500–1,600 GW by 2030, and 2000 GW by 2050 [1]. Reaching these targets would
> imply yearly installations of approximately 100 GW, which raises concerns about whether
> global supply chains can meet the requirements [9].

> Globally, the cumulative installations of wave power reached only 30 MW by 2024 [10],
> but are projected to reach hundreds of GW by 2050 [11].

> For example, the Cirata floating PV plant in Indonesia has 192 MW PV installed on a
> hydropower reservoir [12]. In China, 166,000 panels have been installed on floaters on
> an artificial lake created during mining [13].

The move worth copying is the third sentence in the first quote: a **derived implication**
from cited numbers, which earns the paper its question.

## 19. Defining a term

> Co-located or hybrid offshore energy parks refer to integrated systems where two or more
> renewable technologies (e.g., wind, wave, floating solar) are deployed within a shared
> offshore area and connected to common infrastructure, such as export cables, substations,
> and shared maintenance logistics. This contrasts with traditional single-source parks
> that operate independently.

> In the following analysis, "all locations" refers to the full set of feasible offshore
> sites (n = 22,713), while "co-located" or "mixed" refers to the subset of locations where
> the cost-optimal solution includes two or more technologies with nonzero capacity shares.

Frame: *X refers to Y … This contrasts with Z.* Definitions are given where they are
needed, including inside the results.

## 24. Explaining an anomaly before a referee finds it

> The PV capacity factors are lower than commonly reported global values [76]. This
> discrepancy is primarily due to the assumption of horizontal panel orientation without
> tilt-angle optimization, which is expected to underestimate PV capacity factors relative
> to optimally tilted systems but avoids overestimating performance given the uncertain
> impact of wave-induced motions on OFPV systems [34].

Frame: *X is lower/higher than commonly reported values [n]. This discrepancy is primarily
due to Y, which is expected to Z but avoids W.*

## 25. Enumerating findings inside a paragraph

## 27. Describing a sensitivity analysis

> The sensitivity analysis has been performed by both increasing and decreasing the CapEx
> for one technology (wind power, wave power, OFPV, grid connection costs) by 25% and
> running the optimization again while maintaining the CapEx of the other variables
> constant. These variations can be interpreted as representing uncertainty in future cost
> development, including conservative and optimistic learning pathways for emerging
> technologies. This results in eight sensitivity cases.

And the mechanism behind the largest mover, explained rather than reported:

> The explanation for this change lies in the fact that with the CapEx increase, the number
> of locations where a mix is preferred decreases. With the CapEx increase, locations
> initially cost-optimal for PV–wind installations are now cost-optimal for PV-only
> installations, and therefore fewer locations with high solar resources are included in
> the subset of mixed locations.

## 29. Placing a driver paragraph in the discussion

The full shape, from a three-driver discussion:

> First, the study confirms that economies of scale are the most powerful lever for cost
> reduction. The base scenario presents a small-scale production from field test results,
> which led to 15,000 m3/day (approximately 56 kg/hour or 1.3 tons/day) across ten vertical
> wells, resulting in an LCOH of $6.82 per kilogram. Importantly, scaling production
> capacity by a factor of six to 336 kg/hour (8 tons/day) reduces the LCOH by more than
> 60%. This result emphasizes the nonlinear nature of hydrogen cost formation, where
> capital-intensive components are rapidly diluted as throughput increases. Hence achieving
> commercial scale is critical for competitiveness.

Topic claim → base numbers → key comparison flagged *Importantly* → interpretation →
one-line takeaway.
